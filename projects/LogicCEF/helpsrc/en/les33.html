The potentiometer

Goal: set a voltage with a potentiometer, see that a load makes that voltage drop, and dim an LED with it.

▶ Open this example

Setting a voltage

A potentiometer is a voltage divider in one part. The whole 1000 Ω is between A and B. The wiper L splits it into two parts. With the wiper at 30 %, R(A–L) = 300 Ω and R(L–B) = 700 Ω. Point at the potentiometer: the hint shows both parts.

As long as nothing is connected to the wiper, the voltage on the wiper is:

UL = 12 V × (100 % − position)

  1. Leave the switch open. At 50 % the voltmeter shows 6.0 V.
  2. Drag the blue slider to the left. At 25 % it is 9.0 V, at 0 % (the wiper against A) the full 12 V.
  3. Drag it to the right: at 100 % the wiper is against B and it is 0 V.

A load makes the voltage drop

  1. Set the slider back to 50 % and switch on. The voltmeter drops from 6.0 to 4.6 V.
  2. The branch with the LED is now in parallel with the part L–B. That makes the resistance below the wiper smaller, so the part A–L gets a bigger share of the voltage.

So a voltage divider only gives the calculated voltage as long as you draw hardly any current from it.

Dimming an LED

  1. Keep the switch on and slowly drag the wiper to the left. The LED gets brighter.
  2. At 0 % the full 12 V is on the wiper and the LED gets 21 mA: full light. Point at the LED to see it.
  3. The 470 Ω resistor is still needed: without it, far too much current would flow through the LED at 0 %.

Assignment: when does the LED come on?

A red LED only conducts above 1.8 V. At which wiper position does it start to light? Work it out with the formula above first, then try it with the switch on.

Solution

12 V × (100 % − position) = 1.8 V gives 100 % − position = 15 %, so position = 85 %. At 85 % there is exactly 1.8 V on the wiper and the LED does not conduct yet. At 84 % it starts, with a very small current of 0.2 mA.

In real life: the volume knob of a radio, the dimmer of a lamp and the joystick of a game console are potentiometers. A joystick has two: one for left–right and one for forward–back.
