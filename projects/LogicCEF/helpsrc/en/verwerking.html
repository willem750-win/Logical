Processing

Processing components take signals from the inputs, work on them and pass the result on to the outputs.

Logic gates

A gate has inputs on the left and an output on the right. The symbols in the program carry the Dutch names EN (AND), OF (OR) and NIET (NOT).

In the schematic symbols (IEC and ANSI) you recognise the NAND and NOR gates by the small circle (inversion) at the output. The XOR gate has the sign =1 in IEC and an extra curve at the inputs in ANSI.

Tip: by combining gates you can make any logic function. A NAND gate, for example, does the same as a NOT gate after an AND gate.

Flip-flop

A flip-flop remembers one bit: the output Q stays 0 or 1, even when the inputs turn off again. The second output Q̅ (not-Q) is always the opposite of Q. Right-click to choose the type (the block must not be locked). A new flip-flop starts with Q = 0.

TypeInputsWhat does it do?
SRS RS (set) makes Q 1, R (reset) makes Q 0. S and R on together is really forbidden: in Logic R wins.
DD CAt the moment the clock C turns on, Q takes the value of D. After that D may change without Q changing.
JKJ C KAt the moment C turns on: J and K off = Q stays, J on = Q becomes 1, K on = Q becomes 0, J and K on together = Q toggles.
TTEvery time T turns on, Q toggles: 0, 1, 0, 1, …

The clock input C has a small triangle in the block. The flip-flop only reacts to C turning on (the edge), not while C stays on. Try it in lesson 27.

Build it yourself: four small panels

For each example, click Flip-flop, place it in the middle of the panel and choose the type with the right mouse button. Put the inputs on the left and the lamps on the right. Connect everything with Connection (see Connections) and then start the simulation.

SR flip-flop: an alarm that keeps going

  1. Put two push buttons on the left: the top one is “alarm”, the bottom one “reset”.
  2. Connect the top push button to S and the bottom one to R.
  3. Put a lamp on the right and connect it to Q.

What do you see? Briefly press “alarm”: the lamp comes on and stays on when you release it. Only “reset” switches it off again. This is how an alarm remembers that a door was open for a moment. This example is worked out in lesson 27.

D flip-flop: capturing a value

  1. Put a switch on the left and connect it to D (the data: what must be stored).
  2. Put a push button below it and connect it to C (the clock: when it must be stored).
  3. Put a lamp on the right and connect it to Q.

What do you see? Switch the switch on: the lamp stays off. Only when you press the push button does Q take over the position of the switch and the lamp comes on. Switch the switch off again: the lamp stays on until you press again. So the D flip-flop takes a “photo” of D at the moment of the clock. This is how a computer stores a bit in its memory: D says what, the clock says when.

JK flip-flop: four options in one

  1. Put two switches on the left: connect the top one to J and the bottom one to K.
  2. Put a push button between the two switches and connect it to the clock C.
  3. Put a lamp on the right and connect it to Q.

What do you see? Choose a position of J and K and then press the push button. Nothing happens until that press:

JKAfter a press on the push button (C)
00Q stays as it was
10Q becomes 1: the lamp comes on
01Q becomes 0: the lamp goes off
11Q toggles on every press: on, off, on, …

So the JK flip-flop can do everything: remember, set, reset and toggle. With J and K both on it works as a T flip-flop. Put a NOT gate from J to K (K is always the opposite of J) and it works as a D flip-flop with J as D.

T flip-flop: dividing by two and counting

  1. Put a push button on the left and connect it to T.
  2. Put a lamp on the right and connect it to Q.

What do you see? The lamp toggles on every press: the first press on, the second off, the third on again, … Put a pulse generator on T instead of the push button and the lamp blinks half as fast as the pulses: the T flip-flop divides by two.

A counter from 0 to 3: put a second T flip-flop to the right of the first and connect Q̅ (not-Q) of the first to T of the second. Put a lamp on Q of each flip-flop: the lamp of the first counts 1, the one of the second counts 2. Press four times: 1, 2, 3 and back to 0. In lesson 27 you can open this counter straight away.

Counter block

Counts the pulses on its count input and passes the count on in binary on four outputs.

ConnectionMeaning
TeCount input: every pulse moves the counter one step on.
ReReset: sets the counter back to 0.
8 4 2 1The count as a binary number (0 to 15).

Connect the outputs to lamps or to a display to see the count. A push button or pulse generator on the count input makes the counter run.

Step counter

Counts pulses in decimal and shows the count itself on a four-digit display (0 to 9999). Useful for counting steps, parcels or laps, for example from 1 to 1000.

ConnectionMeaning
TCount input: every pulse adds one to the count.
RReset: sets the count back to 0. While R is on, the counter does not count.
QOutput: on while the end value is reached, for example to a lamp, a buzzer or an input of LogicRun.

Right-click the block (it must not be locked) to choose the end value: 10, 100, 1000, 9999 or another number. At the end value the counter stops. With Repeat switched on, the next pulse starts again at 1, so it keeps counting from 1 to the end value. Set to 0 resets the count by hand. The step counter is a processing block: it needs no power supply.

Tip: a limit switch on the person connected to the count input counts how often the person walks back and forth.

Stopwatch

Measures time in minutes and seconds and shows it on a display (MM:SS, up to 99:59). With output Q you switch something after a set time: a lamp that comes on after 30 seconds, a motor that runs for 1 minute ...

ConnectionMeaning
SStart: the time runs while S is on. When S goes off, the time stops (pause); when S comes on again, it continues.
RReset: sets the time back to 00:00. While R is on, the time does not run.
QOutput: on as soon as the end time is reached.

With the right mouse button (the block must not be locked) you choose the end time: 00:10, 00:30, 01:00, 05:00, 10:00 or another time (as mm:ss, for example 02:30). At the end time the stopwatch stops. If you switch on Repeat, it starts again at 00:01: Q then gives a signal of one second after every end time. The colon blinks while the time runs. The stopwatch is processing: it needs no power supply.

Tip: put a switch on S and a lamp on Q: after the end time the lamp comes on. Set the stopwatch to Repeat and connect Q to the count input of a step counter, and it counts the minutes.
