Switch or push button

Goal: know the difference between a switch, which stays where you put it, and a push button, which only lets current through while you press (a pulse).

Two kinds of control

  • A switch is like the light in your room: you turn it on and it stays on until you turn it off again.
  • A push button is like a doorbell: the bell only rings while you press the button. Let go, and the circuit is open again. With water: a tap with a spring, like a drinking fountain at school.

In Logic it is the same part: right-click the switch › Type › Switch or Push button. The push button draws a small bridge above the contacts; pressing pushes the bridge onto the contacts.

▶ Open this example

Both circuits are connected to the same source. Follow the path of the current with your finger: from the + terminal, through the switch or the push button, through the lamp, and along the top back to the − terminal.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation.
  2. Click the switch and let go: the upper lamp stays on. Click again: it goes out.
  3. Click the push button and keep the mouse button pressed: the lower lamp is on. Let go: it goes out at once.

Exercise

For each example, decide whether you need a switch or a push button: the doorbell, the light in the classroom, the horn of a car, a fan, the button of a lift. Then change the push button in the example into a switch and see what is different.

Solution

Push button: the doorbell, the horn and the lift button (you only give a short signal, a pulse). Switch: the light in the classroom and the fan (they must keep working when you let go). After right-click › Type › Switch the lower lamp stays on when you let go.
