The OR gate

Goal: make an output work when at least one input is on.

▶ Open this example

A real example: the doorbell

A house has a bell button at the front door and at the back door. Whichever button you press, the bell must ring. That is an OR gate:

Front door (O1)Back door (O2)Bell (buzzer)
000
011
101
111

Compare with the AND gate

Put the table next to the one of lesson 4. With AND everything must be on, with OR one is enough. Only when both inputs are off is the output of the OR gate off.

Exercise

Replace the OR gate in this example with an AND gate and try both buttons. When does the bell ring now? Is that still a good doorbell?

Solution

With an AND gate the bell only rings when someone at the front door and someone at the back door press at the same time. With one mouse that is not even possible. For a doorbell the OR gate is the right choice.
