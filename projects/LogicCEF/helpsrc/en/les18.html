Playing a melody with LogicRun

Goal: let LogicRun play a song on a music box. Each step is a note, the time of the step is how long the note sounds. Then you make music yourself.

▶ Open this example

What do you see?

  • A switch on DIN1 (function on/off): the music on or off.
  • The LogicRun block with 61 steps and a step time of 125 ms.
  • On the right, in the output: a music box with 8 piano keys. On the left there are 8 inputs, one per key.
  • The switch hangs on the + rail, the music box on the − rail; see Power supply.
  • The wires: UIT0 to do, UIT1 to re, and so on up to UIT7 to do' (the high do).

Music in steps

A step switches one output on: that note sounds. The time x column says how long. With a step time of 125 ms, time 4 is a quarter note (one beat, half a second) and time 8 a half note (two beats). A step without an output is a rest: then it is silent.

The same note twice in a row? Then the output simply stays on and you hear one long note. That is why there is a short rest in between: 3 ticks the note and 1 tick silent, together 4 again. This is how the song starts:

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and switch on: you hear Twinkle, twinkle, little star and the keys light up.
  2. Open the LogicRun window (double-click the block) and watch the steps follow the music.
  3. No sound in the classroom? Right-click the music box and switch Sound off: the keys still light up.
  4. Switch off: the music stops. Switch on again and the song starts from the beginning.

Exercise 1: faster and slower

Make the song play faster, and then slower.

Solution

Change the Step time in the LogicRun window. With 100 ms it goes faster, with 200 ms slower. All notes change together, because their time is in times the step time.

Exercise 2: a chord

At the end of the song, let three notes sound together: do, mi and sol.

Solution

Choose the last step (the rest) and tick UIT0, UIT2 and UIT4. Give it time 8. Three notes together are called a chord; do-mi-sol is the chord of do (C major).

Exercise 3: your own song

Make Frère Jacques: do re mi do, do re mi do, mi fa sol (long), mi fa sol (long). Or invent a melody of your own with the 8 notes.

Solution

On an empty panel, place a switch, a LogicRun block and a music box, and make the wires. Use + step to make these steps: do 4, re 4, mi 4, do 3, rest 1, do 4, re 4, mi 4, do 4, mi 4, fa 4, sol 8, mi 4, fa 4, sol 8 and at the end a rest of 8. Note: after the fourth note (do) comes another do, hence the short rest. LogicRun has at most 64 steps: enough for a short song.
