The relay: switching a second circuit

Goal: switch a separate circuit on and off with a small control signal, the way a relay does in a car, a washing machine or an alarm system.

▶ Open this example

Two circuits

  • The control circuit (bottom): the 5 V source, the push button and the coil of the relay (the two terminals at the bottom). When you press, about 80 mA flows through the coil.
  • The load circuit (top): the 12 V source, the contact of the relay and a lamp. The + of the source goes to C, the common contact. From the NO contact a wire goes to the lamp, and the lamp goes back to the − of the source.

Each circuit has its own source: the two circuits do not touch. The coil operates the contact with an electromagnet: that is the dotted line in the symbol. So a small 5 V button switches a 12 V lamp. In a car a light switch turns on the heavy headlights through a relay. How a relay works inside is shown in Magnetism 4.

The relay has a changeover contact: at rest C is connected to the NC contact, when the coil pulls in to the NO contact.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and press the push button: the coil turns green, the blade snaps to the NO contact and the lamp lights.
  2. Release it: the blade falls back to the NC contact and the lamp goes out.
  3. Set the bottom source (the control circuit) to 2 V with the slider and press again.

Exercise

1. Why does the relay not pull in when the control circuit gets only 2 V? From which voltage does it work?

2. Set the source back to 5 V. Connect a second lamp to the NC contact (and back to the − of the top source). When does which lamp light?

Solution

1. The coil has 60 Ω and pulls in from 35 mA. At 2 V only 2 / 60 = 33 mA flows: just too little. From 2.5 V (42 mA) the relay pulls in.

2. Without pressing, the lamp on the NC contact lights; when you press, it goes out and the lamp on the NO contact lights. So with one relay you choose between two devices.
