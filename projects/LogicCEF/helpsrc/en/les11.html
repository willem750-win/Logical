The relay: switching a second circuit

Goal: switch a separate circuit on and off with a small control signal, the way a relay does in a car, a washing machine or an alarm system.

▶ Open this example

Two circuits

  • The control circuit: the bottom source feeds the left + rail. The push button sits on that rail and its wire goes to R+, the coil of the switching relay. The coil sits with R− on the − rail next to the relay, which is connected to the − terminal of the bottom source.
  • The load circuit: the top source feeds a second 5 V + rail. The contact of the relay sits on that rail with R1. From R2 a wire runs to the lamp, and the lamp sits on the − rail on the right.

Each circuit has its own source: the two circuits do not touch. The coil operates the contact with an electromagnet: that is the dashed line in the symbol. This way a small button can switch a big device. In a car, a light switch turns on the heavy headlights through a relay.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and press the push button: the LED of the relay turns red, the contact closes and the lamp lights up.
  2. Release it: the contact opens and the lamp goes out.
  3. With its slider, set the top source (the load circuit) to 1 V and press again.

Exercise

Why does the lamp stay off when the source of the load circuit is at 1 V, even though the coil still works? Then set the source back to 5 V and add a buzzer that comes on together with the lamp.

Solution

The contact passes on the voltage of R1. 1 V is less than 2 V, so for the lamp it means “off” (see lesson 10). The coil still works, because it gets its signal from the push button, which sits on the rail of the other 5 V source. For the buzzer: put it with its right side against a − rail and run a second wire from R2 to its input. An output may have several wires.
