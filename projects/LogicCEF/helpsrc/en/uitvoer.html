Outputs

Output components show the result of the circuit. They have one or more inputs on the left, to which you connect the wires from the processing part.

An output component has no separate −: it is invisibly connected to ground. See Where are the plus and the minus? If there are power rails on the panel, plug the outputs in on the left of a − rail (ground).

Lamp

$1

LED: right-click a lamp and choose Colour › Red LED, Green LED or Orange LED. The lamp then becomes an LED in that colour and otherwise works just like a lamp. An LED that is off shows a light shade of its colour. Colour › Lamp turns it back into an ordinary lamp.

Relay

A relay is a switch operated by an electromagnet. On the left is input R to the coil, on the right the changeover contact with the terminals R1 (top) and R2 (bottom).

  • Input off: the coil is not energised and the contact is at rest.
  • Input on: the coil is energised, the red LED turns green and the contact switches over.

The relay shows how a small control signal switches a contact. R1 and R2 are only labels on the symbol: you cannot connect a wire to them. To really switch something with the contact, use the switching relay.

Switching relay

A relay whose contact you can really use: the coil operates a switch that turns a second circuit on and off, for example a lamp or a buzzer.

ConnectionMeaning
R+ (bottom)coil input: the control signal, for example from a gate
R− (right)other side of the coil, to the − rail (with power rails)
R1 (top left)contact input: connect a switch or another source here, or plug the relay onto a + rail on its left: the contact then gets the voltage of that rail
R2 (top right)contact output: from here you run a wire to a lamp, buzzer, ...

While the coil is energized (the LED turns red), the contact is closed and R2 passes on the signal and the voltage of R1. When the coil is not energized, R2 is off, whatever is on R1. You start a wire at R2 like at any output: click the block of R2 with the wire button. If a wire already starts there, click the short line with the arrow just before R2.

Buzzer

Sounds as long as the input is on. Handy as an alarm.

Motor

A motor with two inputs: L makes it turn left (anticlockwise), R right (clockwise). You see the rotor turn and a green arrow shows the direction.

  • Both off: the motor stands still.
  • L and R both on: the motor does not turn and gets a red rim with a !. In reality that would cause a short circuit or damage: make sure L and R are never on together.
  • With power rails the motor, like other outputs, sits with its right side against a − rail (or uses a power wire). Without power it does not turn.

With LogicRun you can make the motor turn according to a program, for example with the Motor left/right example: UIT0 to L, UIT1 to R.

Person

A person who walks left and right across a floor. You place him with the Person button in the component bar, below the voltmeter. His inputs are on the right: L makes him walk left, R right. At both ends of the floor there is a limit switch with a lamp; their outputs are on the left: S1 is on when he is all the way to the left, S2 when he is all the way to the right.

  • Both off: he stands still. L and R both on: he stays where he is and a red ! appears.
  • Connect S1 and S2 to an input of LogicRun (for example DIN3 and DIN4): a step can then wait until the person has arrived. See lesson 16.
  • The person needs no power supply. After opening a panel he is always on the left. Right-click and choose Back to the left (S1) to put him back yourself.

7-segment display

The display has one input. What it shows depends on what you connect to it:

  • the display output of a counter or a memory: the value as a digit or letter, 0 to 9 and then A to F;
  • any other output (push button, switch, gate, sensor...): 1 when the output is on, 0 when it is off.

Voltmeter

The voltmeter shows the voltage on its +, measured against ground, for example 3.3 V. Connect a wire to the + on the left, or place the meter between a + rail and a − rail. See Power & voltmeter.
