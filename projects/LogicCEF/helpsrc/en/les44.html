The servo motor

Goal: a servo motor does not turn round and round, but to an angle that you choose with a voltage. Next to it you see an ordinary motor (a fan) that simply turns as long as current flows.

▶ Open this example

Three wires

  • + (red) and − (black) power the motor. It works from 4 V.
  • S (orange) is the control signal. The voltage on S chooses the angle: 0 V gives 0° (arm to the right), 2.5 V gives 90° (straight up) and 5 V gives 180° (to the left).

Here the potentiometer is between + and −, with the wiper on S: the potentiometer is a voltage divider that gives a voltage from 0 to 5 V.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and slide the potentiometer. The arm follows: slide to the left and the arm turns to the left.
  2. The orange triangle on the scale shows the requested angle. The arm does not jump there at once, but turns at a fixed speed. Put the wiper in the middle: S becomes 2.5 V and the arm points straight up (90°).
  3. Set the source to 3 V. Now the servo gets too little power: the arm stays put, even when you move the potentiometer. Set the source back to 5 V.
  4. Close the switch: the fan turns as long as current flows. Raise the source voltage: it turns faster (100 rpm per volt). An ordinary motor does not choose an angle: it just turns.

Task: a barrier

Remove the wire from the wiper to S. Put a switch between the + rail and S. What does the arm do when you switch it on and off? Can you also connect S to the output of a gate?

Solution

With the switch closed S is at 5 V: the arm turns to 180°. When the switch is open S drops to 0 V and the arm turns to 0°. That is a barrier with two positions. A gate also gives 0 or 5 V, so a gate can drive the servo just as well: e.g. an AND gate that only opens the barrier when two conditions are true.

In real life: a servo does not get a voltage on S, but a short pulse every 20 ms: 1 ms is 0°, 2 ms is 180°. Inside, a potentiometer on the shaft measures the real angle, and a circuit turns the motor until it matches. Servos are in robot arms, model aircraft and remote-controlled cars. With an Arduino board Logic can also make a real servo turn along: see Arduino board.
