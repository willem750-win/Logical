The stepper motor

Goal: a stepper motor turns in small, precise steps. You switch on the coils one by one, and each time the shaft turns a fixed angle further. So you always know exactly how far it has turned.

▶ Open this example

How does it work?

In the middle is a small magnet that can turn: the rotor. The red tip is the north pole. Around the rotor are four coils: A at the top, B on the right, C at the bottom and D on the left. When current flows through a coil it becomes an electromagnet and pulls the rotor towards it. Each coil is 100 Ω and pulls from 20 mA.

  • Each coil has its own terminal on the left (A, B, C, D). The other end of all coils comes together in the common terminal −, which is connected to the − rail.
  • On the right you see the shaft with a pointer, the angle and the number of steps. This motor makes 48 steps per revolution: each step is 360 / 48 = 7.5°.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and press button A: coil A turns red and the north pole of the rotor points up.
  2. Now press B, C and D in turn. Each time the rotor jumps to the next coil: it turns clockwise and the counter goes 1, 2, 3. The pointer of the shaft moves 7.5° further each time.
  3. Press A again, then B ... After 48 steps the shaft has turned exactly once.
  4. Press in the reverse order: D, C, B, A. Now it turns anticlockwise and the counter counts down.
  5. Release all buttons: the rotor simply stays where it was.

Task: half steps and a mistake

Right-click push buttons A and B and choose Type › Switch (stays put). Switch on A, then A and B together, then only B. What do you see? And what happens if you switch on A and C together?

Solution

With A and B together two neighbouring coils pull: the rotor stops exactly between them. That is a half step (3.75°): the motor turns more finely and smoothly. With A and C together two opposite coils pull: the rotor does not know which way to go and stays put. The motor shows a red exclamation mark.

In real life: stepper motors are in printers, 3D printers and cameras, wherever something has to move precisely. The small stepper motor from a starter kit (28BYJ-48) has a gearbox and makes about 2048 steps per revolution. An Arduino drives it with four outputs through a driver module (ULN2003), because a pin gives too little current. Right-click the stepper motor to choose 4, 48 or 200 steps per revolution.
