Learning path

Twenty-five lessons, from a switch with a lamp to counters, memory, power supply, the relay, programming with LogicRun, controlling a motor, counting, making a person walk, driving a cart making music and counting to 1000. Each lesson has a worked example, an explanation and an exercise to take it further yourself.

How it works: read the lesson, click Open this example and start the simulation to see the example work. Then try the exercise. Only open the solution when you are stuck.

New: starting from energy (pilot block)

Five lessons that start at the basics: what is energy, how does it flow, and how do you switch it? With the comparison with water and always a closed circuit from the source, through the load, back to the source. From lesson 4 on, everything sits between a + rail and a − rail.

  1. Energy flows like waterSource, wire and lamp as pump, pipe and water wheel: voltage and current.
  2. The circuit must be closedA switch in the way out and one in the way back.
  3. Switch or push buttonOn for good or only while you press: a pulse.
  4. The distribution line: railsSupply and return for many loads; the currents add up.
  5. The transistor on railsA small current switches a large one, neatly between the rails.

Magnetism

Magnets and coils: what a magnet does, how a current makes a magnet, how to make it stronger and where you meet the electromagnet.

  1. What is magnetism?North pole and south pole, field lines and the compass; like poles repel, unlike poles attract.

Logic and power supply

  1. Your first circuitA switch, a wire and a lamp.
  2. Push button or switch?A short signal or a lasting signal.

  4. The AND gateTwo-hand control of a press.

  12. The relaySwitching a second circuit with a push button and a switching relay.

For advanced users: programming with LogicRun

Lessons 12 to 22 are an extension for those who know the basics (lessons 1 to 11). You program with LogicRun and make a motor, a person and a cart move or play a melody with a program in steps. The teacher can hide the LogicRun, Person, Cart and Music box buttons until the class is ready (see Settings).

  1. Programming with LogicRunA traffic light with a program in steps.
  2. Controlling a motorMaking a motor turn left and right with LogicRun.
  3. A packing machineCounting boxes with LogicRun and the counter.
  4. Counting in binaryLamps and a display show the same number.
  5. A person walking back and forthLimit switches tell LogicRun where the person is.
  6. A cart on a roadLogicRun counts the posts along the road.
  7. Music with LogicRunOne step per note: LogicRun plays a song on a music box.
  8. Counting to 1000The step counter counts pulses and switches on a lamp at the end value.
  9. Switching on timeThe stopwatch measures time and switches on a lamp when the end time has passed.
  10. A traffic light with pedestrian buttonLogicRun controls a traffic light: green for the cars until a pedestrian presses the button.
  11. A barrier gate at a car parkA stopwatch and a NOT gate: the barrier opens after the ticket button and closes by itself.

Current and voltage

After the power supply of lesson 10 you calculate here with current, voltage and resistance. You do not need LogicRun.

  1. Ohm’s lawMore lamps, more current: the voltage drops and the fuse blows.
  2. A burnt contactA poor contact takes voltage away and gets warm.
  3. Lamps in parallelEvery lamp gets the full voltage, the currents add up.

Calculating with gates

After the gates of lesson 3 to lesson 6 you add numbers with gates and remember with flip-flops here. You do not need LogicRun.

  1. The half adderAn XOR gate and an AND gate add two bits.
  2. The flip-flop remembersAn SR flip-flop remembers a button press; two T flip-flops count.

Electronics

After the power supply of lesson 23 to lesson 25 you build with resistors, a potentiometer, LEDs, a capacitor and diodes here, and turn AC voltage into DC voltage. Finally you switch and amplify with a transistor, and build a night light and a temperature alarm with an LDR and an NTC.

  1. The LED and the capacitorA series resistor protects the LED; a capacitor makes it go out slowly.
  2. The voltage dividerTwo resistors in series share the voltage of the source.
  3. Resistors in parallelEach branch draws its own current; the currents add up.
  4. LED coloursEach colour a different forward voltage, and the right series resistor.
  5. PowerP = U × I: a strong lamp in series with a weak one, and a resistor that gets hot.
  6. The potentiometerSet a voltage with the wiper, see a load make it drop, dim an LED.
  7. The diodeCurrent in one direction: forward and reverse direction.
  8. AC voltageA sine on the oscilloscope; period, frequency and peak value.
  9. Half-wave rectificationOne diode lets half of each wave through.
  10. The Graetz bridgeFour diodes use both halves; a capacitor smooths the voltage.
  11. The transistor as a switchA small base current switches on a large current through a lamp.
  12. Amplifying with a transistorIc = β × Ib, between off and saturated.
  13. A night light with an LDRA light-dependent resistor and a transistor: the LED switches on when it gets dark.
  14. A temperature alarm with an NTCA temperature-dependent resistor switches on the LED when it gets too warm.
  15. A gate drives a transistorAn AND gate lights a 12 V lamp through a transistor: logic and power together.

Practising with a check

After the lessons you can get to work yourself with the exercises: you get an assignment, build the circuit and Logic checks whether it is right.

Linux: there too the help opens in the built-in browser and the Open this example button works. If the ceflib folder is missing, the help opens in the normal web browser and that button does not work. Open the examples with Open panel, in the folder panels/lessen.
Tip: an example is a copy. Feel free to change it: when you Save, Logic asks for a new name, so the example itself stays unchanged.
