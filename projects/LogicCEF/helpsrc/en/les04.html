The AND gate

Goal: make an output work only when all inputs are on.

▶ Open this example

A real example: two-hand control

A press in a factory may only work when the operator presses both buttons, one with each hand. That way a hand can never be under the press. The AND gate does exactly that:

Left hand (A1)Right hand (A2)Press (lamp)
000
010
100
111

Two inputs give four possibilities. Only in the last row is the output on.

Why switches? In a real press they are push buttons. With one mouse you can only hold down one button at a time in Logic. That is why two switches represent the two hands here.

Try it

Start the simulation. Turn on one switch first, then the other, and watch when the lamp is lit. Go through the four rows of the table this way.

Exercise

The press may only work when the safety gate is closed too: three conditions. An AND gate has only two inputs. How do you solve that?

Solution

Use two AND gates in a row. The first gate gets the two hands. Its output goes, together with a third switch (the gate), to the second AND gate. The lamp is connected to the second gate: it is only lit when all three are on.
