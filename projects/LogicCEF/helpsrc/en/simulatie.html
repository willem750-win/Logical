Simulation

In the simulation you use the circuit like a real one: you operate the inputs and watch the outputs react, without accidentally moving anything.

Where are the plus and the minus?

In a real electric circuit the current flows from the + of a source (a battery, for example), through the switch and the lamp, and back to the −. In Logic you build it the same way: the circuit has a real supply, with a source at the top, a red + rail on the left and a blue − rail on the right.

  • The current flows in a closed circuit: from the + rail through the switch and the lamp with two terminals, and from the right terminal of the lamp back to the − rail. See lesson 1.
  • A wire carries a signal. A red wire carries voltage: with 2 V or more the signal is on (1), otherwise it is off (0), always measured against the − rail (ground, 0 V).
  • Gates, counters, flip-flops, the memory and LogicRun have no + and −: they get their own supply invisibly (+5 V and ground) and give 5 V on their output. That is enough for a lamp for 5 V or a buzzer.
  • Old panels with the earlier lamp, buzzer or relay with one input still work: the − of those components is invisibly connected to ground. That is why a single wire is enough there.

Real logic diagrams are drawn the same way: the supply lines of the gates are rarely shown, because they are the same for all gates.

You can read more about the source, the rails and the voltmeter, which measures the voltage, in Power & voltmeter.

Starting and stopping the simulation

Click Start simulation. The button turns red (). Click it again to stop the simulation and continue editing.

What changes during the simulation?

  • The component bar, the editing buttons, the rulers and the status bar are hidden. This gives the panel more room.
  • The guide lines are turned off.
  • Components can no longer be moved. You operate them with the mouse: click a switch to open or close it, and hold a push button down for as long as it has to stay closed.

After the simulation everything returns as it was, including the guide line setting.

Tip: circuit not working as expected? Stop the simulation, hold the mouse over the components and check their state in the status bar, or put a lamp with two terminals for 5 V between a wire and the − rail to check the signal.
