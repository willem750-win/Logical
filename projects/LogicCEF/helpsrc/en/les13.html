Controlling a motor with LogicRun

Goal: make a motor turn left and right with a program, as in a roller shutter, a garage door or a small cart.

▶ Open this example

What do you see?

  • On the left a switch on DIN1. DIN1 has the function on/off: as long as the switch is on, the program runs. Switch it off and the program stops and all outputs go off.
  • In the middle the LogicRun block.
  • On the right a motor: UIT0 goes to L (turn left), UIT1 to R (turn right).

The program

StepOnMotortime xDuration (step time 500 ms)
1UIT0turns left42 seconds
2-stands still10.5 second
3UIT1turns right42 seconds
4-stands still10.5 second

Why the short stop between left and right? A real motor has to come to a standstill before it changes direction, otherwise it gets a hard jolt.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and switch the switch on: the motor turns left, stops for a moment and then turns right.
  2. Switch it off: the motor stops at once. Switch it on again: the program starts again at step 1.
  3. Double-click the block and follow along in the LogicRun window: the current step is light blue.

Exercise

In step 2, switch on the circles of UIT0 and UIT1. What happens to the motor? Then undo it and make the motor turn left for 3 seconds and right for 1 second.

Solution

With L and R on at the same time the motor does not turn and gets a red rim with a !. In reality that would cause a short circuit or damage, so a good program never switches L and R on together. For 3 seconds left, set time x of step 1 to 6 (6 × 500 ms); for 1 second right, set step 3 to 2.
