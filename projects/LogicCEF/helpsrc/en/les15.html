Counting in binary with LogicRun

Goal: understand how a binary number works: four lamps and a display show the same number, in two different ways.

▶ Open this example

What do you see?

  • A switch on DIN1 (function on/off): counting on or off.
  • The LogicRun block with 16 steps: step 1 is 0, step 2 is 1, … step 16 is 15. Each step lasts 1 second.
  • Four red LEDs on UIT0 to UIT3. Each LED has its own value: UIT0 = 1, UIT1 = 2, UIT2 = 4, UIT3 = 8.
  • A display on T1: it reads UIT0 to UIT3 as one number (see Displays).

How does a binary number work?

A computer only knows on (1) and off (0). Still, four lamps make 16 numbers: add up the values of the lamps that are lit. Write them down with the 8 on the left and you get the binary number.

After 9 the display runs out of digits: 10 to 15 become the letters A to F. Such a number is called hexadecimal: one character for each group of four bits.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and switch the switch on: LogicRun starts counting.
  2. Look at which LEDs are lit and add up their values. Does it match the display?
  3. Open the LogicRun window (double-click the block) and click Pause. Step moves on one number. The T1 column shows the display digit for each step.
  4. After 15 it starts again at 0. Switch the switch off: everything stops.

Exercise 1: a countdown

Make the display count down from 9 to 0, and then stop.

Solution

Use - step to remove steps until 10 are left. Click the T1 column of step 1 and choose 9, for step 2 8, … up to step 10 0. LogicRun switches on the right LEDs itself. Untick Repeat: after the last step the program stays where it is.

Exercise 2: counting to 15 with two digits

The display shows A to F, but we would rather write 10 to 15. Put a second display to the left of the first one and connect it to T2. Make it count from 00 to 15.

Solution

T2 is the tens digit, T1 the units digit. Leave steps 1 to 10 as they are (T2 = 0, T1 = 0 to 9). In steps 11 to 16 choose 1 for T2 and 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 for T1. The display now shows 10 to 15. In the window you can see that T2 = 1 switches on output UIT4: it has the value 1 for the second digit.
