Your first circuit

Goal: connect a switch to a lamp with a wire, and make the circuit work with the simulation.

▶ Open this example

Build it yourself

  1. Choose Panel choice › New standard panel and delete what you do not need, or start with an empty panel.
  2. Set up the power: a + rail on the left, a − rail on the right and a source at the top, with a wire from each terminal of the source to its rail (see Energy 4: the distribution line).
  3. In the Power group of the component bar, click the switch and put it next to the + rail.
  4. In the same group, click the lamp with two terminals and put it on the right, a little before the − rail.
  5. Click Connection. Draw a wire from the + rail to the left terminal of the switch, a wire from the right terminal of the switch to the left terminal of the lamp, and a wire from the right terminal of the lamp to the − rail. See Connections.

Try it

  1. Click Start simulation.
  2. Click the switch: the lamp goes on. Click again: the lamp goes off.

A wire carries a signal: on (1) or off (0). A signal is energy: with 2 V or more on the wire it is a 1. A red wire means “on”. The energy comes from the source through the + rail, goes through the lamp and back through the − rail: the circuit is closed (see Energy 2).

Exercise

Make the same switch turn two lamps on and off at the same time.

Solution

Put a second lamp on the panel and draw a second wire, also from the right terminal of the switch; the second lamp also gets a wire to the − rail. Several wires may start from one output: each wire carries the same signal.
