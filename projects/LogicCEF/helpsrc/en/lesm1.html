What is magnetism?

Goal: know what a magnet does, what the north pole and the south pole are, how field lines show the magnetic field, how a compass works and when magnets attract or repel each other.

Magnets around you

A magnet attracts iron without touching it: a paper clip jumps towards the magnet. Nickel and cobalt are attracted too, but wood, plastic, glass, copper and aluminium are not. You find magnets on the fridge door, in the catch of a cupboard door or a bag, in a loudspeaker, in an electric motor and in a bicycle dynamo.

Two poles: N and S

Every magnet has two poles: a north pole (N, red here) and a south pole (S, blue here). The magnet is strongest at the poles; in the middle it attracts almost nothing.

  • Hang a bar magnet freely and it turns until its north pole points to the north. That is where the name comes from.
  • A compass needle is such a small magnet that can turn freely. Its north pole (the red tip) points north.
  • You never get a single pole. Break a magnet in two and you have two smaller magnets, each with an N and an S.

The magnetic field and the field lines

Around a magnet there is a magnetic field: the space in which it exerts a force. You cannot see it, but you can make it visible. Put a sheet of paper on a magnet and sprinkle iron filings on it: the grains line up. Those are the field lines.

  • Outside the magnet the field lines run from N to S. The small arrows show that direction.
  • Where the lines are close together, the field is strong: at the poles.
  • A compass always points along the field line where it stands: the red tip follows the arrows, away from the north pole and towards the south pole.

In Logic the field line sheet does the same as the iron filings: put magnets on it and it draws the field lines.

Attract and repel

Below the sheet lies a second magnet. Drag it onto the sheet, next to the first one. When the magnets come close, a yellow arrow on each magnet shows the force it feels.

  • Put the north pole of one opposite the south pole of the other: the arrows point towards each other. They attract, and the field lines run from one magnet to the other.
  • Turn the second magnet round so that two north poles face each other: the arrows point apart. They repel, and the field lines bend away from each other.
Remember: unlike poles attract (N and S), like poles repel (N and N, or S and S).

The Earth is a big magnet

With no magnet nearby, the compass points north: in Logic that is up on the panel. The Earth itself behaves like a giant magnet. Because unlike poles attract, near the geographic North Pole there is actually a magnetic south pole: it attracts the north pole of the compass needle.

Exercise

Put the two magnets in one line with a small gap between them, so that they attract. Put the compass in the gap. Where does it point? Then turn one magnet round. What changes in the field lines in the gap, and in the compass?

Solution

When they attract, the field lines run straight and close together from the north pole of one magnet to the south pole of the other. The compass points along that line, towards the south pole. Turn one magnet round and they repel: in the middle of the gap the two fields almost cancel, the field lines bend aside, and the compass turns sideways (or to the Earth’s north, if it stands exactly in the middle).
