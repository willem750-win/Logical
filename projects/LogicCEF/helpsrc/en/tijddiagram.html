Timing diagram

A timing diagram shows how the inputs and outputs of your panel change over time, like a logic analyzer in a real lab. Every input and output gets a line: high is on, low is off.

How to use it

  1. Open a panel or build a circuit with inputs (switches, push buttons, pulse generators …) and outputs (lamps, buzzers …).
  2. In the title bar, click Timing diagram. The window opens to the right of Logic.
  3. Switch the simulation on and operate the switches and push buttons. Ten times a second Logic draws the state of every line; the picture scrolls to the left.

Reading the diagram

  • A, B, C … (blue) are the switches, push buttons, DIP switches and sensors, from top to bottom, just as in the truth table. P1, P2 … are the pulse generators.
  • Below the grey line are the outputs (green): L = lamp or LED, B = buzzer, R = relay, M = motor (with L or R for the direction) and D = display (with the digit).
  • One box is one second; at the bottom you see the time since the recording started.
  • The window remembers the last 5 minutes.

The buttons

  • Pause freezes the picture. You can then look back with the scroll bar below the diagram. Continue follows the time again.
  • Clear empties the diagram and starts again at 0 seconds.
  • If you add or remove a component, the diagram also starts again.
Tip: a timing diagram is most useful for circuits that change over time: a flip-flop, a counter or a pulse generator. There a truth table does not tell everything, because the output also depends on what happened before.

Try it

Open lesson 27 (flip-flop), switch the simulation on and press S and R a few times. In the timing diagram you see that the LED stays on after a short press on S, until you press R: that is the memory of the flip-flop.
