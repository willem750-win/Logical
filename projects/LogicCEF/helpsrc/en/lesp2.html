The circuit must be closed

Goal: discover that current only flows in a closed circuit: from the source, through the lamp and back to the source. A switch is a tap that opens or closes the circuit.

A tap in the pipe

If you close a tap in the fountain of lesson 1, the water stops everywhere: not only behind the tap, but in the whole pipe. The water can no longer go round. It does not matter whether the tap is in the pipe to the water wheel or in the pipe back to the pump.

A switch does the same with current. Mind the words:

  • switch closed (on): the circuit is closed, current flows;
  • switch open (off): the circuit is open, no current flows.

A tap is just the other way round: an open tap lets water through. That is why for a switch we prefer to say closed and open as for a bridge: you can cross a closed drawbridge.

▶ Open this example

Try it

  1. Start the simulation. Both switches are open: no electrons, no light.
  2. Close only the lower switch (the way out). Does current flow now? Look at the electrons before the lamp and after the lamp.
  3. Now also close the upper switch (the way back).

Exercise

Say in your own words why the lamp stays off when only the lower switch is closed. There is voltage on the lamp, isn’t there? Then think: here the lamp must light when switch 1 and switch 2 are closed. Where have you seen that before?

Solution

The current cannot get back to the source: the circuit is open. Without a way back nothing flows anywhere, not even in the piece of wire that is connected to the source, just as water does not go round with a closed tap in the return pipe.

Two switches one after the other (in series) work as an AND: only when the one and the other are closed does the lamp light. That is exactly what an AND gate does. A gate is a circuit that lets current through or holds it back.
