The strength of a coil

Goal: find out what the strength of an electromagnet depends on: the current, the number of turns and an iron core.

Three knobs to turn

In lesson 2 a coil with a current became a magnet. How strong is that magnet? It depends on three things:

  • The current I: twice the current gives a field twice as strong.
  • The number of turns N: every turn adds its share. Twice as many turns with the same current also gives a field twice as strong. Together this is called ampere-turns: N × I.
  • An iron core: iron contains a great many tiny magnets lying every which way. The field of the coil lines them all up in the same direction, and then they help. The field becomes much stronger: five times in Logic, often hundreds of times in a real electromagnet.

A magnet pulls a piece of iron towards where the field gets stronger: close to a pole the force is large, a little further away it quickly gets smaller. So a nail close to the coil comes first.

Try it

The example starts with a coil of 100 turns without a core. To the right of the north pole lie three nails, each further away. Point at a nail with the mouse: the hint says what percentage of the necessary force there is at that spot. From 100 % it slides to the coil.

  1. Start the simulation and close the switch. About 98 mA flows. Does a nail move? Look at the hint of the first nail.
  2. Right-click the coil and choose 200 under Turns, then 400. What happens?
  3. Open the switch and put the nails back: right-click a nail, Put back. (With the current on it jumps straight back to the coil.)
  4. Set the turns back to 100 and tick Iron core. Close the switch. Also try 200 and 400 turns with the core.
  5. Each time, look at the field line sheet: the stronger the coil, the more and the denser the field lines.

More turns help, but the iron core helps most: 100 turns with a core is already stronger than 400 without.

At 6 V only half the current flows (about 47 mA). The ampere-turns are halved: 400 × 0.047 A is about the same as 200 × 0.094 A. So the coil pulls two nails, just like 200 turns with a core at 12 V.
