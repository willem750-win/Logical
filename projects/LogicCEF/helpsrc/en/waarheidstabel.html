Truth table

A truth table shows which lamps are on for every position of the switches. Logic makes this table itself: it puts the switches and push buttons of your panel in every position, one by one, and looks which lamps and buzzers come on.

How to use it

  1. Build a circuit with switches or push buttons, gates and lamps or buzzers, or open an example.
  2. In the title bar, click Truth table.
  3. The window shows the table and follows your panel: if you add, remove or move a component or wire, Logic works out the table again by itself.
  4. Flip a switch (in the simulation): the row with the red border is the current position and moves along.

Reading the table

  • A, B, C … (light blue) are the inputs, from top to bottom: switches, push buttons, DIP switches, the light sensor and the heat sensor. If two are at the same height, the left one comes first.
  • L1, L2 … (light yellow) are the lamps and LEDs, also from top to bottom; B1, B2 … are the buzzers, R1 … the relays and switching relays.
  • A motor (M1 …) shows L (turns anticlockwise), R (clockwise) or 0 (stopped). A display (D1 …) shows the digit on it; - means dark (no power).
  • The pulse generator, the limit switch and the LogicRun components (person, cart …) are left out: they depend on time or on a program.
  • A 1 means on, a 0 off. An output that is on turns green.
  • Each row is one position. With 2 switches there are 4 rows, with 3 switches 8 rows; the table handles up to 6 switches (64 rows).

With Copy you put the table on the clipboard, to paste it into a word processor or spreadsheet. After the table is made, the switches are back as they were.

Note: a truth table suits circuits with gates: there the outputs only depend on the position of the switches right now. If there is a flip-flop, counter or other component with a memory on the panel, the output also depends on what happened before. Logic then warns you, and the table does not always apply.
