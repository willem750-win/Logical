Power and voltage

Goal: see where the current comes from, measure voltage with a voltmeter, and understand why a logic input needs enough voltage.

▶ Open this example

Power rails

In the previous lessons the power came invisibly from the background. With power rails you make it visible:

  • the red + rail supplies the inputs (here the push buttons), with a voltage you choose yourself;
  • the blue − rail is ground (0 V), to which the outputs (the lamps) are connected.

The top push button is on a 5 V rail, the bottom one on a 1 V rail.

Measuring voltage

The voltmeter is connected to the wire of the bottom push button and measures the voltage against ground: 1.0 V. Yet the lamp stays off. A logic input only works from about 2 V: below that the signal counts as “off”.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and press the bottom push button: the voltmeter shows 1.0 V, the lamp stays off.
  2. Stop the simulation. Right-click the bottom + rail and choose Voltage › 3.3 V.
  3. Start again and press: the voltmeter shows 3.3 V and the lamp is lit.

Exercise

Find the lowest voltage in the menu at which the bottom lamp is still lit. What happens to a lamp that you pull off the − rail?

Solution

From 2 V the lamp is lit; at 1 V and 0 V it is not. A lamp that is not plugged into a − rail gets a grey dot and is never lit: without a connection to ground no current can flow.
