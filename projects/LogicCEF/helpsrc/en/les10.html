Power and voltage

Goal: see where the current comes from, measure voltage with a voltmeter, and understand why a logic input needs enough voltage.

▶ Open this example

Source and power rails

In the previous lessons the power came invisibly from the background. Here you make it visible with a power circuit:

  • a source (battery): with the slider you choose a voltage from 0 to 12 V;
  • the red + rail gets that voltage through a wire from the + terminal and feeds the inputs (here the push buttons);
  • the blue − rail is ground (0 V). The − terminal of the source is connected to it and the outputs (the lamps) are connected to it.

The top source is set to 7.5 V, the bottom one to 1.5 V. Each source feeds its own + rail; they share the − rail.

Measuring voltage

The voltmeter hangs on the wire of the bottom push button and measures the voltage against ground: 1.5 V. Still the lamp stays off. A logic input only works from about 2 V: below that the signal counts as “off”.

Try it

  1. Start the simulation and press the bottom push button: the voltmeter shows 1.5 V, the lamp stays off.
  2. Drag the slider of the bottom source to 3 V. You may do this during the simulation.
  3. Press again: the voltmeter shows 3.0 V and the lamp lights up.

Exercise

Use the slider to find the lowest voltage at which the bottom lamp still lights up. What happens when you delete the wire between the − terminal of a source and the − rail?

Solution

From 2 V the lamp lights up; at 1.5 V and lower it does not. Without the wire to the − rail the circuit is not closed: the + rail of that source drops to 0 V and turns grey, and the push button on that rail no longer works. No current can flow without a way back to the source.
